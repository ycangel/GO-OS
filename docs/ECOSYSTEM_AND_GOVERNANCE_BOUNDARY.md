# GO OS Ecosystem and Governance Boundary

**Status:** release-canonical boundary for v0.5

**Scope:** project roles, public-upstream governance, commercial packaging and
self-application. This document does not create a company, foundation,
partnership, shareholding, contract or other legal relationship. Executed legal
documents prevail over this project-role description.

## The boundary

GO OS follows a public-upstream / enterprise-offering separation.

| Role | Current boundary | Not implied |
|---|---|---|
| **GO OS** | The maintained public upstream: Constitution, public specifications, schemas, Skills, tests and reference code | A registered foundation; a commercial vendor; ownership of customer data |
| **思思** | The intended enterprise packaging and service layer: supported distributions, integration, operations, service levels and customer-specific delivery | Authority to silently close the public upstream; automatic rights to customer or community data |
| **PIX Moving** | The current project design assigns PIX the role of practitioner, contributor and minority shareholder in the enterprise company; the actual ownership position is governed only by executed corporate agreements | Control of upstream governance by virtue of being a practitioner, contributor or shareholder |
| **GO Society** | The project community and alpha self-application reference instance used to test GO OS against its own work | Proof that the complete cognitive loop has operated; a separate legal entity merely because the name is used |

The commercial company may describe a compatible offering as **Powered by GO**
when the relationship to the public upstream and any proprietary additions are
clear. It should not imply that commercial features, service commitments or
customer-specific IP are already part of the public project.

## Governance invariants

1. **Public upstream remains real.** Public interfaces, releases and accepted
   contributions are maintained as upstream work, not as a marketing mirror of
   a private product.
2. **Human responsibility remains named.** AI may create candidates, analyses
   and proposals; humans retain purpose, judgment, approval and accountability.
3. **Commercial rights do not equal constitutional authority.** Equity,
   contracting or customer status does not by itself grant authority to change
   GO OS constitutional boundaries.
4. **Customer cognition does not flow upstream by default.** Customer data,
   evidence, learning and derived assets require contractual authority,
   consent, effective de-identification and human review before any reuse.
5. **Compatibility is explicit.** Enterprise extensions and adapters identify
   the public version they implement and disclose material divergence.
6. **Fork and succession remain possible.** Licensing and repository history
   must allow the public project to continue if one steward or commercial
   provider changes direction.

## Decisions still requiring formal instruments

The public repository does not substitute for:

- entity registration or company constitutional documents;
- trademark ownership and usage rules;
- contributor-license or developer-certificate policy;
- shareholder, IP-assignment or related-party agreements;
- customer data-processing, confidentiality and service agreements; or
- a documented maintainer decision and appeal process.

Until those instruments exist and are published where appropriate, describe
them as open governance work—not as completed legal infrastructure.
