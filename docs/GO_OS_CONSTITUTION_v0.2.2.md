# GO OS Constitution v0.2.2

This document is the **canonical source of truth** for GO OS constitutional principles. Other documents may summarize these principles but should not redefine them independently.

## 1. Human Sovereignty
Humans retain sovereignty over purpose, value arbitration, legitimate authority, major resource commitments and responsibility for irreversible consequences. Delegation does not erase accountability.

## 2. Effective Control
Human sovereignty is meaningful only when the accountable human has sufficient observability, comprehension and intervention rights. Ceremonial accountability without effective control is invalid governance.

## 3. Machine Agency
Machines and agents should receive as much autonomous action space as demonstrated capability, evidence quality, observability, reversibility and risk justify. The objective is maximum responsible agency, not maximum automation.

## 4. No Self-Expansion of Authority
An agent may not autonomously expand its own authority, resource limits or constitutional permissions, nor remove required human control. Authority must be granted by a legitimate grantor and remain revocable according to explicit rules.

## 5. Reality as Final Arbiter
Plans, leaders, models, metrics and policies are representations or hypotheses. Reality has final arbitration. Evidence must remain traceable, challengeable and open to contradiction.

## 6. Mission over Task
GO OS optimizes for evidence-verifiable state transitions, not activity volume. Tasks are implementation details inside a Mission, not the primary organizational object.

## 7. Progressive Formalization
Governance rigor should scale with consequence, irreversibility, uncertainty and aggregate exposure. Low-risk reversible work should not be burdened with unnecessary formalism; high-impact action requires explicit contracts and evidence.

## 8. Exceptions Are First-Class Information
Exceptions reveal where assumptions, authority, capabilities, sensing or policies no longer match reality. Recurrent exceptions must trigger structural learning rather than endless escalation or firefighting.

## 9. Learning Means Convergence
Iteration speed is not learning by itself. Organizational learning must improve future decisions or capabilities and demonstrate evidence-correcting convergence rather than thrashing.

## 10. Self-Revision Is Bounded
Organizations should be able to update missions, policies, capabilities, skills, structures and operating rules. Operational rules may evolve faster than constitutional rules. Constitutional change requires explicit human sovereignty and stronger evidence.

## 11. Aggregate Exposure Matters
Multi-agent systems must be governed at system level, not only per action. Budget, concurrency, correlated risk, conflict and cumulative commitments must remain inside explicit aggregate limits.

## 12. GO OS Is Falsifiable
GO OS itself is a hypothesis. Every material GO OS intervention should state what evidence would support it and what evidence would show it is wrong. A valid conclusion may be that GO OS adds more cost than value in a given domain.

## Constitutional priority
When rules conflict, prefer:

1. human sovereignty, law and safety;
2. explicit legitimate authority;
3. high-quality reality-grounded evidence;
4. mission purpose;
5. framework consistency.

**Reality tests outrank loyalty to the framework.**
